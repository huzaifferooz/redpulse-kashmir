The flow

Three steps, from the first sign of trouble to a stabilized patient.

No call centre queue, no guessing which hospital to head toward. The request goes straight to whoever is closest and available.

01

Request

Open the app, describe what's happening, and your location is shared automatically — even if you can't give a clear address.

02

Match

The nearest verified responder in the network is alerted and confirms how long they'll take to reach you.

03

Care

They arrive, stabilize the patient at home, and coordinate a hospital transfer if the case needs one.

Kashmir's geography breaks most emergency-response models. We built around it instead.

A single central ambulance dispatch works for a grid city. It works less well when the address is "past the walnut orchard, second lane after the shrine," or when the district hospital is an hour away and the road closes for winter.

Red Pulse is a network of nearby responders rather than one fleet — so the person who reaches you first is genuinely close, not just next in a queue.

  • Old-city lanes

    Dense, narrow gullies where large ambulances struggle to reach the door — matched instead to responders who know the mohalla on foot or by bike.

  • Hill and border villages

    Settlements far from the nearest district hospital get matched to the closest available responder, not the closest hospital-based one.

  • Seasonal road closures

    Snow-blocked routes and landslide season are treated as a routing input, not an exception the system breaks on.
